## 2. Article 1: Artificial Intelligence Governance (ID: 37)

* **Preserved URL:** `https://omniwiremedia.com/article/artificial-intelligence-governance-new-international-standards-adopted-for--117`
* **Current Title:** *Artificial Intelligence Governance: International Standards Became Law in 2026*
* **Revised Title:** **AI Governance Laws in 2026: EU AI Act Legal Mandates, ISO/IEC 42001 & Compliance Roadmaps**
* **Revised Summary:** *A clear breakdown of statutory EU AI Act penalties versus voluntary ISO/IEC 42001 certification, high-risk compliance timelines, and enterprise governance frameworks.*

### Direct Answer Opening (Featured Snippet Target)
> Under the European Union’s Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation EU 2024/1689, entered into force 1 August 2024), AI governance became legally binding statutory law rather than voluntary corporate policy. Organizations deploying prohibited AI systems face statutory fines up to **€35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover** (whichever is higher), while non-compliance with high-risk system obligations carries penalties up to **€15 million or 3%**.
>
> In contrast, **ISO/IEC 42001 (published December 2023)** is a voluntary, certifiable international management system standard—not statutory law. While ISO/IEC 42001 carries no government fines, enterprise audit committees use its Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) framework to document technical due diligence, mitigate liability, and satisfy conformity assessment requirements mandated by statutory regulators.

### Factual Source Citations
1. **European Parliament & Council:** [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council (AI Act)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401689), published in the Official Journal of the European Union on 12 July 2024 (statutory phased enforcement from February 2025 through August 2026).
2. **International Organization for Standardization:** [ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html), published December 2023.

## 3. Article 2: SaaS Net Revenue Retention (NRR) Optimization (ID: 118)

* **Preserved URL:** `https://omniwiremedia.com/article/how-saas-net-revenue-retention-nrr-optimization-in-2026-changed-the-rules-for-customer-success-playbooks-and-usage-based-pricing-this-quarter-658`
* **Current Title:** *SaaS Net Revenue Retention in 2026: Usage-Based Pricing Rewrote the Customer Success Playbook*
* **Revised Title:** **SaaS Net Revenue Retention (NRR) in 2026: Usage-Based Pricing & Retention Playbooks**
* **Revised Summary:** *How consumption-based and hybrid pricing models rebuilt SaaS net revenue retention (NRR) benchmarks following generative AI seat compression, backed by BVP and OpenView data.*

### Direct Answer Opening (Featured Snippet Target)
> Net Revenue Retention (NRR) measures the percentage of recurring revenue retained from an existing cohort of customers over a 12-month period, factoring in expansion revenue, downgrades, and churn. Following generative AI seat-compression—where automated agents allow 5 engineers or support reps to handle workloads previously staffed by 50—per-seat subscription models suffered structural revenue decay.
>
> According to the **Bessemer Venture Partners (BVP) State of the Cloud Report (published May 8, 2024)**, top-tier private cloud NRR normalized from 2021 highs (130%+) to a disciplined median of **110% to 115%**, while public cloud NRR stabilized near **112%**. To defend net retention, B2B vendors pivoted toward consumption metrics (API calls, workflow compute, throughput), which **OpenView’s 2024 SaaS Pricing Benchmark (published October 2024)** found delivered net retention rates 8 to 10 percentage points higher than pure per-user licenses.

### Factual Source Citations
1. **Bessemer Venture Partners:** [State of the Cloud 2024](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/state-of-the-cloud-2024), published May 8, 2024 (cloud ARR expansion rates and AI seat-compression data).
2. **OpenView Partners:** [2024 SaaS Pricing Benchmarks](https://openviewpartners.com/), published October 2024 (usage-based vs per-seat retention differentials).

## 4. Article 3: Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Migration (ID: 112)

* **Preserved URL:** `https://omniwiremedia.com/article/post-quantum-cryptography-migration-nist-standards-kyber-768-and-tls-1-3-implementation-roadmap-409`
* **Current Title:** *Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration: NIST Standards, Kyber-768 and the TLS 1.3 Roadmap*
* **Revised Title:** **Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration: NIST FIPS 203, ML-KEM-768 & TLS 1.3 Roadmap**
* **Revised Summary:** *Technical analysis of hybrid post-quantum TLS 1.3 key exchange (X25519MLKEM768), NIST FIPS 203 standards, packet fragmentation trade-offs, and migration roadmaps through 2029.*

### Direct Answer Opening (Featured Snippet Target — "Zero Latency" Removed)
> Post-quantum cryptography (PQC) replaces public-key algorithms vulnerable to Shor’s algorithm on cryptanalytically relevant quantum computers (CRQCs) with quantum-resistant mathematical lattices. Standardized by NIST on **August 13, 2024 under FIPS 203**, ML-KEM (formerly CRYSTALS-Kyber) establishes quantum-proof key encapsulation mechanisms.
>
> Unlike classical elliptic-curve keys (such as X25519’s compact 32-byte public key), post-quantum algorithms entail real physical network trade-offs rather than "zero latency": **ML-KEM-768 requires 1,184-byte public keys and 1,088-byte ciphertexts**. In TLS 1.3 handshakes, this packet enlargement approaches typical 1,500-byte network MTU boundaries, introducing occasional TCP segment fragmentation and 1 to 5 milliseconds of additional handshake overhead. However, hybrid deployments combining classical X25519 with ML-KEM-768 protect against "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" state espionage while preserving existing security properties against classical attacks.

### Factual Source Citations
1. **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST):** [FIPS 203 Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism Standard](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final), published August 13, 2024.
2. **Cloudflare Telemetry & IETF:** [Post-Quantum for All: TLS Handshake Performance and RFC Standards](https://blog.cloudflare.com/post-quantum-for-all/), published October 2023, updated August 2024.
